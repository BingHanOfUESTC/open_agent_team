## Slide 2: He Sold One Painting in His Lifetime. Today, His Work Defines Modern Art.

- **Facts**:
  - Only one confirmed sale during lifetime: *The Red Vineyard*, sold for 400 francs to Anna Boch at the 1890 Les XX exhibition, Brussels.
  - *Portrait of Dr. Gachet* sold for $82.5 million at Christie's in 1990 (world record at the time).
  - ~860–900 oil paintings and ~1,100 drawings in 10 years (roughly one artwork every 36 hours).
  - Johanna van Gogh-Bonger published his letters (1914) and organized posthumous exhibitions.
  - His paintings hang in MoMA, Musée d'Orsay, and the Van Gogh Museum.
- **Source**: source_pack.md §8.1 (lifetime recognition — Met Museum, Wikipedia: The Red Vineyard); §8.2 (posthumous fame — Met Museum, Wikipedia); §8.4 (output statistics — Wikipedia, Met Museum, Britannica); §8.6 (auction records — Wikipedia: List of works).

## Slide 3: He Tried Being an Art Dealer, a Teacher, and a Preacher. None of It Worked.

- **Facts**:
  - Born March 30, 1853, in Zundert, Netherlands (Brabant region); eldest of six children of Theodorus van Gogh, a Protestant pastor.
  - Age 16: Apprenticed to Goupil & Cie in The Hague; worked in London (1873–1875) and Paris (1875–1876).
  - Became solitary after a romantic rejection in London, 1874.
  - Worked as a language teacher, lay preacher, and bookseller in Dordrecht (1877).
  - 1878–1879: Missionary work in the Borinage; gave away all possessions; dismissed by church authorities for "too-literal interpretation of Christian teaching."
- **Source**: source_pack.md §1.1 (birth & family — Britannica); §1.2 (early jobs — Britannica, Met Museum Heilbrunn Timeline); §1.3 (Borinage period — Britannica).

## Slide 6: "They Have Tilled the Earth Themselves with the Same Hands They Are Putting in the Dish."

- **Facts**:
  - Painted April–May 1885 in Nuenen, after 40+ studies of peasant heads during winter 1884–1885.
  - Van Gogh's first large-scale, multi-figure composition — his first attempt at a "masterpiece."
  - Depicts a peasant family sharing potatoes by lamplight; Van Gogh's stated intent: "they have tilled the earth themselves with the same hands they are putting in the dish."
  - Dark earthy palette characteristic of Dutch period; coarse application of paint; influenced by Millet and the Barbizon school.
  - Van Gogh said the painting should "smell of bacon, smoke, and potato steam."
- **Source**: source_pack.md §2.3 (Nuenen period — Met Museum, Britannica); §2.4 (The Potato Eaters — Met Museum, Van Gogh Museum); §9 (key quotes — Van Gogh Letters).

## Slide 7: In Two Years, Paris Transformed Everything He Knew About Color.

- **Facts**:
  - Moved to Paris February 1886; lived with brother Theo (art dealer at Boussod, Valadon et Cie).
  - Studied at Fernand Cormon's studio; met Émile Bernard, Paul Gauguin, Camille Pissarro, Georges Seurat, Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec, Paul Signac, and Edgar Degas.
  - Witnessed Impressionist works firsthand and Neo-Impressionist innovations by Seurat and Signac — lightened his palette dramatically.
  - Began collecting Japanese ukiyo-e prints obsessively — amassed hundreds.
  - Painted more than 20 self-portraits in Paris, using himself as a laboratory for complementary color contrasts.
  - *Self-Portrait with Straw Hat* was painted on the reverse of an earlier dark peasant study.
- **Source**: source_pack.md §3.1 (move to Paris — Met Museum); §3.2 (Impressionism discovery — Met Museum, Britannica); §3.3 (artist encounters — Met Museum, National Gallery); §3.4 (self-portraits — Met Museum); §3.5 (Japonisme — Wikipedia Japonaiserie, Van Gogh Letters).

  - **Painting**: Self-Portrait with Straw Hat, 1887. Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York.
  - **Source URL**: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Van_Gogh_Self-Portrait_with_Straw_Hat_1887-Metropolitan.jpg

## Slide 8: Two Years Changed Everything: From the Dark North to the Color of Light.

- **Facts**:

  - Two paintings made barely two years apart demonstrate complete stylistic transformation.

## Slide 10: He Painted Sunflowers to Decorate a Room for a Friend Who Would Soon Betray Him.

- **Facts**:
  - Rented the Yellow House in May 1888; painted it in September.
  - Painted the Sunflowers series (multiple versions, August 1888) specifically to decorate the guest room for Paul Gauguin's anticipated arrival.
  - Sunflowers versions are now at National Gallery London, Van Gogh Museum, Neue Pinakothek Munich, and Sompo Museum Tokyo.
  - Also painted The Bedroom (3 versions, October 1888) — a deeply personal interior where color conveys rest and tranquility.
  - Produced the Flowering Orchards series (14 paintings, March–April 1888), capturing Provençal spring.
- **Source**: source_pack.md §4.1 (arrival — Met Museum); §4.2 (Yellow House — Met Museum, National Gallery); §4.3 (key works table — Met Museum, National Gallery, Wikipedia); §4.4 (Gauguin's visit context — Met Museum, Britannica).

## Slide 11: He Didn't Paint the Night Black. He Painted It in Blues, Greens, and Gold.

- **Facts**:
  - *Café Terrace at Night* (September 1888) — Kröller-Müller Museum, Otterlo: first painting with a starry night background; painted on location using gaslight; no black used for the sky.
  - *Starry Night Over the Rhône* (September 1888) — Musée d'Orsay, Paris: depicts the Rhône at night with gas lighting and the Great Bear constellation.
  - *The Night Café* (September 1888) — Yale University Art Gallery: deliberately unsettling interior with clashing reds and greens to express "the terrible passions of humanity."
  - Van Gogh's innovation: night was not the absence of light but a different kind of light, rich with color and emotion.
- **Source**: source_pack.md §4.3 (key works table — Met Museum, National Gallery, Wikipedia); §7.3 (color as emotional expression — Met Museum, National Gallery).

## Slide 12: Gauguin Arrived on October 23. By December 23, Van Gogh Had Cut Off His Own Ear.

- **Facts**:
  - Gauguin arrived in Arles on October 23, 1888; they lived and painted together for ~9 weeks, including side-by-side paintings of Les Alyscamps.
  - Gauguin was domineering and critical; Van Gogh was emotionally volatile and craved collaboration.
  - December 23, 1888: After a heated argument, Van Gogh cut off part of his left ear with a razor; he wrapped the severed ear and delivered it to a woman at a nearby brothel.
  - Gauguin left Arles abruptly; Van Gogh was hospitalized. The dream of the Studio of the South died.
  - After returning from hospital in January 1889, Van Gogh painted again immediately, including Self-Portrait with Bandaged Ear.
- **Source**: source_pack.md §4.4 (Gauguin's visit — Met Museum, Britannica); §4.5 (ear incident — Britannica, Met Museum); §4.6 (hospitalization — Met Museum).

## Slide 14: He Painted the Entire Cosmos from a Room in an Asylum.

- **Facts**:
  - Painted June 1889, during confinement at Saint-Paul-de-Mausole asylum, Saint-Rémy-de-Provence.
  - Depicts the view from his east-facing asylum window just before sunrise, with an idealized village added from memory.
  - The cypress tree (a dark flame connecting earth and sky) was not in the actual view — added from memory.
  - The swirling sky is Van Gogh's most famous technical achievement: thick impasto brushwork transforming the night sky into a living, breathing presence.
  - Now at MoMA, New York — one of the most reproduced and recognized artworks in human history.
  - **Size**: 73.7 cm × 92.1 cm (oil on canvas).
- **Source**: source_pack.md §5.3 (key works: The Starry Night — Met Museum, MoMA); §5.2 (productivity, asylum grounds — Met Museum).

## Slide 16: He Averaged a Painting a Day. He Had 70 Days Left.

- **Facts**:
  - Discharged from Saint-Rémy in May 1890; moved to Auvers-sur-Oise (~30 km north of Paris) to be near Theo.
  - Placed under the care of Dr. Paul Gachet, a homeopathic physician and amateur painter. Van Gogh assessed Gachet as "sicker than I am" (letter to Theo, July 10, 1890).
  - Spent a few days in Paris with Theo, his wife Jo, and their newborn son Vincent Willem.
  - Produced over 70 paintings in approximately 70 days.
  - Key works: Portrait of Dr. Gachet (two versions, June 1890), The Church at Auvers, Daubigny's Garden, Wheatfield with Crows.
- **Source**: source_pack.md §6.1 (move to Auvers — Met Museum, Death of VG Wikipedia); §6.2 (Dr. Gachet — Met Museum, Death of VG Wikipedia); §6.3 (final burst of creativity — Met Museum, Death of VG Wikipedia); §6.4 (changing mood — Death of VG Wikipedia).

## Slide 17: Two Weeks Before the End: "I Feel Like a Failure. I Am in a Mood of Almost Too Much Calm."

- **Facts**:
  - "I feel like a failure... And the prospect grows darker, I see no happy future at all." — Letter to Theo, July 10, 1890.
  - "I am in a mood of almost too much calm, just the mood needed for painting this." — Letter to his mother and sister, circa July 12, 1890.
  - "I am giving my canvases my undivided attention... I am trying to do as well as certain painters whom I have greatly loved and admired." — Letter to Theo, July 23, 1890 (his last letter).
  - In the span of two weeks, Van Gogh moved from declaring himself a failure to speaking of calm focus to pouring his final energies into his canvases.
- **Source**: source_pack.md §6.4 (changing mood, letter excerpts — Death of VG Wikipedia); §9 (key quotes table — Van Gogh Letters project, vangoghletters.org).

## Slide 18: He Walked Into a Wheat Field on July 27, 1890. Two Days Later, He Was Gone.

- **Facts**:
  - Sunday evening, July 27, 1890: Van Gogh walked into a wheat field near Auvers and shot himself in the chest with a revolver.
  - He returned to his inn around 9 PM, climbed the stairs to his room; Dr. Gachet dressed the wound but considered it hopeless.
  - Theo arrived the next afternoon and stayed by Vincent's bedside.
  - Van Gogh's last words to Theo: "La tristesse durera toujours" (The sadness will last forever).
  - Died at approximately 1:30 AM on July 29, 1890 — aged 37.
  - Funeral on July 30, 1890: coffin surrounded by his last canvases and masses of yellow flowers (dahlias and sunflowers).
  - Theo died six months later on January 25, 1891; reburied beside Vincent in 1914 in the small cemetery above the Auvers wheat fields.
  - **Alternative theory**: Naifeh & Smith (2011) proposed possible accidental shooting by teenage boys. This theory is noted but widely disputed by Van Gogh Museum scholars; the presentation treats the consensus suicide narrative as the primary account.
- **Source**: source_pack.md §6.5 (the shooting, death — Death of VG Wikipedia, Met Museum, Britannica); §6.6 (funeral — Death of VG Wikipedia); §9 (last words quote — Death of VG Wikipedia, Van Gogh Letters).
- **Image**: `materials/images/processed/van_gogh_wheat_field_crows_1890.jpg`
  - **Painting**: Wheat Field with Crows, 1890. Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam.
  - **Source URL**: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vincent_van_Gogh_-_Wheatfield_with_crows_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg

## Slide 19: In One Decade, He Reinvented What a Painting Could Be.

- **Facts**:
  - 1880–1883 (The Hague/Drenthe): Self-taught beginnings; watercolors and early oils; instruction from cousin Anton Mauve; dark, moody Dutch realism.
  - 1883–1885 (Nuenen): 40+ peasant head studies; The Potato Eaters — first major composition; dark earthy palette; influenced by Millet and Barbizon school.
  - 1886–1887 (Paris): Lightened palette; Impressionist broken brushstrokes; Neo-Impressionist pointillism experiments; 20+ self-portraits as color studies; Japonisme influence.
  - 1888–1889 (Arles): Full maturation of personal style; bold, vibrant colors; distinctive swirling brushwork; thick impasto; series approach (Sunflowers, Orchards); night scenes revolutionizing color use.
  - 1889–1890 (Saint-Rémy): Continued bold color; thickest impasto of career; emotional and symbolic use of form; cypresses as obelisks, stars as cosmic forces; ~150 canvases.
  - May–July 1890 (Auvers-sur-Oise): Late style — broader, more agitated brushwork; double-square canvases; 70+ paintings in 70 days.
- **Source**: source_pack.md §7 (Artistic Techniques & Style Evolution — Met Museum, Britannica); §7.1 (from dark realism to expressionism); §7.2 (impasto — Met Museum, National Gallery); §7.3 (color as emotional expression — Met Museum, National Gallery); §7.4 (Japonisme — Wikipedia Japonaiserie); §7.5 (series approach — Met Museum).

## Slide 20: Closing Slide — "The Sadness Will Last Forever." But So Does the Art.

- **Facts**:
  - Total output: ~2,100 artworks (~860–900 oil paintings, ~1,100 drawings and sketches, 11+ watercolors) in a single decade.
  - ~820 surviving letters: 652 to Theo, plus letters to sister Wil, artist friends Émile Bernard and Anthon van Rappard, Paul Gauguin, and others.
  - Influence rippled through Fauvism, German Expressionism, and Abstract Expressionism; Matisse, Kirchner, and countless others built on his discoveries.
  - Major collections: Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam (largest); Kröller-Müller Museum, Otterlo; Musée d'Orsay, Paris; MoMA, New York.
  - Core message: Van Gogh's story is not ultimately about mental illness or tragedy — it is about the fierce, life-consuming dedication of an artist who found, in paint, a language for everything he had to say.
- **Source**: source_pack.md §8.2 (posthumous fame — Met Museum, Wikipedia); §8.3 (influence on art movements — Britannica, Met Museum); §8.4 (total output statistics — Wikipedia, Met Museum, Britannica); §8.5 (most famous works — multiple museum websites, Wikipedia); §8.7 (key Van Gogh museums — Wikipedia, Met Museum).
